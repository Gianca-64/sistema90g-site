Practical kitchen quote case

The discount is obvious. The real value is much less clear.

The total can look convincing while materials, appliances, work, exclusions and possible additional costs remain difficult to understand.

Analysis by Gian Carlo Primo · Updated 7 July 2026

Quick answer

Price cannot be judged from the total and the discount alone.

To understand the proposal, you need to know what is actually included: materials, product codes, accessories, appliances, installation, fabrication, delivery and exclusions.

The real-life situation

Two quotes can have a similar total and still describe very different supplies.

The difference may be in hardware, the worktop, accessories, appliance models or work that is not included. Without this information, the comparison remains incomplete.

What needs to be checked

The value comes from what makes up the total.

Materials and codes

Important components need to be identifiable, not described only with generic wording.

Work and fabrication

Cuts, alterations, surveys, installation, delivery and connections need to be clarified before signing.

Exclusions

An excluded item can become an additional cost after the kitchen has already been confirmed.

Limit of this public analysis: without the complete quote, product data sheets and the final kitchen design, it is not possible to establish the economic value of the supply.

The consequence

The risk is discovering after signing what the total did not reveal.

Excluded work, a different accessory or an unidentified component can change the final cost or the result you receive.
