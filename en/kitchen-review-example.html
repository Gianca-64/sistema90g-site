Illustrative scenario

It starts from an existing design, not from a new kitchen design.

Imagine that you have a floor plan, a kitchen design, some measurements, appliance information and a concern about how the composition will work in real use. The review asks what can responsibly be concluded and what still requires confirmation.

Material available

Design, measurements and technical information.

The review uses what is actually available. If an essential piece of information is missing, that absence becomes part of the result.

Starting question

“Will the kitchen really work as it appears to?”

The concern is translated into defined checks: openings, clearances, clashes, appliances and constraints that can be read from the material.

Objective

Separate evidence, risk and uncertainty.

The result does not force a single conclusion when the documents do not support one.

The rule

Not everything has to become an error.

A useful review may confirm a point, identify a critical issue, request another check or state that something cannot be determined. Part of its value is refusing to manufacture certainty.

Example review result

Three different conclusions require three levels of certainty.

VERIFIED

The dishwasher opening affects the clearance shown in the design.

Evidence: in this illustrative scenario, the design and available information allow the position and opening envelope to be considered together.

Possible consequence: while loading or unloading, the circulation route may be occupied and should be considered against the intended use.

Next step: make a conscious decision about whether that behaviour is acceptable before the supply becomes final.

TO CHECK

A fridge close to the wall may require more opening space.

Evidence: the position raises a plausible concern, but the exact appliance information is incomplete.

Missing information: actual door geometry, manufacturer guidance and the final distance from the wall.

Next step: obtain the exact model and installation information and compare them with the real measurement.

NOT DETERMINABLE

Final compatibility of the extraction system cannot be concluded.

Reason: in this illustrative scenario there is not enough information about the route, dimensions and technical requirements.

What we do not do: turn missing information into either approval or rejection.

Next step: obtain the missing information and have the relevant points confirmed by the supplier, installer or qualified professional.

Reading the result

The review result is not a list of faults.

It may contain identified critical issues, possible consequences, consistent elements, missing information and points still requiring verification. Not everything reviewed has to be problematic.

A consistent element is also a result.

When a point appears consistent with the documents available, it can be recorded as such. The aim is not to inflate the number of observations, but to make clearer what genuinely deserves attention before proceeding.

The next step should be clear.

When a critical issue appears or confirmation is missing, the result indicates which information or check would be useful before the next decision.

This example shows the logic of the review result. The actual content always depends on the design, the question and the documentation genuinely available.

What this example does not represent.

It is not a certification, does not include redesign, and does not replace an executive survey, services engineering or checks that remain the responsibility of the retailer, installer or qualified professional.
