Practical kitchen case

The island fits the dimensions. But it can take space away from circulation, seating and openings.

A floor plan can contain the island and still perform poorly during real use, especially when seating, appliances and circulation all occupy the same zone.

Analysis by Gian Carlo Primo · Updated 8 July 2026

Quick answer

It is not enough to check that the island fits the room.

You need to understand what remains around it when doors and appliances are open, when someone is seated and when more than one person moves through the room.

The real-life situation

One person is cooking, one is seated at the island and another needs to cross the room.

If all of these functions occupy the same strip of space, the island can stop working as an operational centre and become a daily obstacle.

What needs to be checked

Useful space is the space that remains during use.

Working clearances

Read the distances with doors, drawers and appliances open, not only with all fronts closed.

Seating

The space behind stools needs to allow someone to sit down, stand up and let another person pass.

Routes

The entrance, dining area, fridge and exit should not all depend on one overly compressed passage.

Limit of this public analysis: without final measurements, actual depths, seating dimensions and full opening dimensions, it is not possible to state that the passages are safe.

The consequence

The risk is ordering an island that works on paper but constrains everything around it.

Correcting the problem later can mean reducing the seating area, changing units, moving appliances or revising the entire composition.
