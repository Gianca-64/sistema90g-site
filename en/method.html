An Italian method for better kitchen decisions

A question does not automatically become an answer. First, it has to be understood.

A design, quote, photograph or measurement can reveal a great deal. It can also leave important questions unanswered.

Developed in Italy · independent checks · human-controlled conclusions

The method separates what we know, what we can verify and what is still missing. Only then does it make sense to decide what the next step should be.

The principle

The service comes after understanding the problem.

Sometimes you need a review. Sometimes you need to compare alternatives. Sometimes a solution needs to be developed. And sometimes the useful next step is simply to obtain a measurement, a product data sheet or confirmation from the relevant party.

The correct outcome is not always to sell a service. It is to identify what is genuinely useful.

From the question to the next step

Six steps. No shortcuts on the important part.

01

OBSERVE

Start with the real question.

What does not look right? What needs to be decided? At what stage is the kitchen?

02

GATHER

Use what actually exists.

Floor plans, designs, quotes, photographs, measurements, orders and product data are read together when they are available.

03

DISTINGUISH

Separate facts, assumptions and missing information.

Documented information is not treated like an assumption. Missing information is declared, not completed with invented data.

04

VERIFY

Look at what happens in real use.

Circulation, openings, occupied space, appliances, ergonomics, maintenance and compatibility are considered together.

05

ASSESS

Find what can change a decision.

Not every detail has the same weight. Critical points, possible consequences and issues that require further checking are made visible.

06

DEFINE THE NEXT STEP

The conclusion needs to be useful.

Correct it, investigate further, ask for confirmation, compare alternatives or do nothing else: the useful next step is stated clearly.

Not every statement has the same certainty

The level of certainty should be visible.

VERIFIED

There is sufficient evidence.

The point is supported by the available material, a measurement, documentation or another relevant source.

TO CHECK

The risk is visible, but confirmation is still missing.

The issue may be plausible, but another measurement, document or check is required before treating it as a conclusion.

NOT DETERMINABLE

Essential information is missing.

When the available evidence is insufficient, Sistema 90G does not manufacture certainty.

A simple example

“The passage is wide enough” is not yet a conclusion.

1

Evidence: the floor plan shows a distance.

2

Question: what happens when the dishwasher, oven or drawer is open?

3

Verification: the space must be assessed in its real-use configuration, not only with everything closed.

4

Conclusion: if a necessary measurement is missing, the correct result may be “TO CHECK”, not “it is fine”.

Tools

Technology helps. It does not make the final decision.

Digital tools and artificial intelligence can help read material, compare information, organise checks and reduce repetitive work.

They do not autonomously decide what the problem is, what matters or what answer you should receive.

Final review and assessment remain human.

Where the method stops

Declaring a limit is part of the verification.

01

Information is missing.

The missing measurement, document or confirmation is identified before a conclusion is made.

02

The retailer or manufacturer needs to confirm it.

Modular compatibility, product availability and supply-specific conditions need confirmation from the party providing the product.

03

A competent professional or installer needs to verify it.

Sistema 90G does not replace specialist technical checks, inspections or certifications that need to be carried out by the relevant qualified or competent professional.
